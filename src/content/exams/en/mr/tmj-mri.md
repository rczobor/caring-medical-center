## Temporomandibular joint MRI (jaw joint MRI)

A temporomandibular joint MRI scan (TMJ MRI or jaw joint MRI) is recommended when a problem is suspected in how the jaw joint works, in its structure or in the surrounding soft tissues, and other imaging (such as X-ray or CT) cannot show it precisely.

The advantage of MRI is that it shows the disc, the joint capsule, the ligaments and the joint fluid in detail, so it is the best method for detecting functional and soft tissue problems.

### Main indications (when it is recommended):

- Clicking, popping or locking of the jaw, especially if it starts suddenly or persists.
- Limited mouth opening, or pain when opening (while chewing or speaking).
- Chronic or recurring joint pain around the ear or the temple.
- Suspected joint dislocation or disc displacement.
- Facial asymmetry, swelling, or a suspected disorder of the chewing muscles.
- After an injury, if soft tissue damage (disc, ligament) is suspected alongside bone changes.
- In rheumatic conditions (such as rheumatoid arthritis or osteoarthritis), to assess the extent of joint damage and inflammation.
