A lumbar spine (lower back) exam shows non-bony changes in the lower section of the spinal column. Among other things, it shows the condition of the intervertebral discs and changes in the bone marrow and vertebrae, and it is also very well suited to follow-up checks after surgery.

### When is a lumbar spine exam worth having?

- muscle weakness or numbness in the legs
- reduced sensation in the legs
- radiating pain that travels toward the legs
- pain along the spine
- sudden lower back pain
- a forced (antalgic) posture in the lower back

If you have these symptoms, we also recommend a consultation with a specialist.

### What conditions can the pain be linked to?

- a herniated disc or disc inflammation
- inflammation of the vertebrae
- narrowing of the nerve root canal and/or the spinal canal
