A cardiac MRI scan gives the following comprehensive information:

- The dimensions of the heart
- The condition and movement of the heart muscle
- How well the heart valves work
- Circulatory disorders of the heart muscle (ischemia)
- Detection of a recent heart attack
- Scars after an earlier heart attack
- Inflammation of the heart muscle (myocarditis)
- Blood clots (thrombus)
- Other abnormalities of the heart or heart muscle, such as congenital heart defects
- Tumors
- The overall function of the heart

Cardiovascular MRI is an ECG-gated (timed to the heartbeat) MRI scan that examines the function and tissue characteristics of the heart.

It shows the size of the heart, the volume of the heart chambers and the thickness of their walls. It gives a picture of any cysts, tissue growths, blood clots (thrombi) formed in the heart, and tumors. We can examine the condition of the pericardium (the sac around the heart), its diseases, and detect any fluid collected inside it.

Cardiac MRI is one of the special imaging methods that can make moving images of the heart. So, much like a heart ultrasound (echocardiography), but in far more detail, it lets us examine the heart. The heart's pumping function, its global function, can be assessed precisely. This matters most in diseases that damage the heart muscle and in planning their treatment.

We can also examine how the heart valves work. With a similar method, we can get a complete picture of the condition of the great vessels (such as the aorta) and the diseases that can be diagnosed in them.

Using T1- and T2-weighted sequences and a contrast agent, it can give a proper assessment of the viability of the heart muscle (for example after a heart attack), inflammatory processes affecting the heart muscle (such as myocarditis) and their consequences (such as sarcoidosis), as well as the various cardiomyopathies (HCM, DCM, ARVC) and storage diseases (iron overload, Fabry disease, amyloidosis).

Viability exams are very important and useful for treatment planning in patients who have had a heart attack.

Overall, cardiac MRI is a useful part of the diagnostic workup: at low risk and without radiation, it can provide valuable information that supports the recovery of cardiology patients.
