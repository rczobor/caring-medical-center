Over the years, medical imaging has become one of the basic tools of modern healthcare. Advances in technology let us "look inside" the body without invasive methods. Below we review the basic methods of medical imaging, how they developed, and the differences between them.

## X-ray and ultrasound

X-ray is one of the oldest imaging methods. Wilhelm Conrad Röntgen discovered X-rays in 1895, and they quickly spread in medical diagnostics. This method records radiation passing through the body, which goes through different tissues to different degrees. X-ray images are especially good at showing bones and other hard tissues.

Ultrasound became popular from the middle of the 20th century. Here sound waves are sent into the body, and an image is formed from how they reflect back. It is mainly used to examine pregnant women's bellies and the heart, but it has many other uses as well.

## CT and MRI

CT, or computed tomography, is based on a combination of X-rays and computer technology. It takes a series of cross-sectional images of the body, which the computer then turns into a three-dimensional image. CT images are more detailed and informative than conventional X-rays, and they are especially good at showing internal organs.

MRI, or magnetic resonance imaging, uses magnetic fields and radio waves to make images of the body's internal structure. MRI began to spread from the 1970s, and today it is one of the most accurate methods, giving some of the clearest images. MRI images show soft tissues especially well, such as the brain or the joints.

## The goal: the most accurate diagnosis possible

The development of medical imaging has improved diagnostic accuracy enormously. Starting from simple X-ray images, today we can make images of the body so detailed that they would once have been unimaginable. This lets medical diagnostics give information about a patient's condition in a much more precise, faster and less invasive way.

But imaging matters not only in diagnostics. These methods often help with planning, monitoring and evaluating treatment too. Because medical imaging is present at almost every level of patient care, it is essential to know and understand these techniques in depth.

Over the past decades, medical imaging has radically changed medicine. The imaging procedures offered by modern technology let us look inside the body more accurately and in more detail, improving diagnostic accuracy and the quality of patient care.
