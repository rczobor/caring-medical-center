Medical technology has developed ever faster over the past decades, and MRI is one of the biggest milestones along the way. While many other imaging technologies are available, MRI has a number of advantages that make it stand out. In this chapter we look at the main advantages of MRI diagnostics and where the technology is used in medicine.

## Advantages

1. No ionizing radiation: Unlike X-ray or CT, MRI does not use ionizing radiation. This means there is no radiation risk, which is especially important for exams that are repeated often, or for younger patients.

2. Excellent contrast between soft tissues: MRI is excellent for examining soft tissues such as the brain, muscles and joints. The contrast in the images makes it easy to identify abnormal changes.

3. Three-dimensional imaging: MRI can create three-dimensional images of the body, which allows more accurate diagnosis and treatment planning.

4. A contrast agent is not always needed: While some MRI scans use a contrast agent to make the images sharper, in many cases it is not needed, which lowers the risk of allergic reactions.

## Where it is used

1. Neurology: MRI scans of the brain and spinal cord are essential for diagnosing neurological conditions, such as multiple sclerosis or brain tumors.

2. Orthopedics: MRI of the joints, tendons and bones helps identify joint damage, injuries and degenerative changes.

3. Cardiology: Cardiac MRI makes it possible to assess the condition of the heart muscle, the structure of the heart chambers and the great vessels.

4. Oncology: MRI can detect tumors early and track the effects of treatment.

5. Gastroenterology: MRI is useful for examining abnormal changes in the liver, pancreas and bile ducts.

6. Gynecology and urology: MRI can identify abnormal changes, for example in the uterus or the prostate.

MRI technology offers unmatched advantages in many areas of medicine. Its exceptional imaging capabilities allow early and accurate diagnosis of diseases, reducing the risks related to treatment and improving patients' quality of life. Future developments in MRI technology may further widen where the method is used and how effective it is in healthcare.
