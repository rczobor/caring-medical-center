Cardiac diagnostics aims to understand the condition of the heart and blood vessels. Over the past decades, magnetic resonance (MR) imaging has become one of the most innovative tools in cardiac diagnostics. Thanks to it, we can gain deeper insight into the structure and function of the heart and into various aspects of the circulatory system.

## The basics of cardiac MRI

Cardiovascular MRI (CMR) gives us detailed, three-dimensional images of the heart and blood vessels. It allows early detection of vascular changes, heart muscle damage or atherosclerosis.

1. Heart structure and function: CMR can show the exact size, shape and function of the heart's ventricles and atria, and can also visualize how the heart valves work.

2. Myocardial perfusion: CMR can also be used to examine the blood supply of the heart muscle, revealing any blocked or narrowed vessels.

## Features of vascular MRI

MR angiography makes it possible to visualize blood vessels, especially arteries. The vessels can be seen clearly even without contrast agents, so atherosclerosis or narrowed vessels are also easy to recognize.

Cardiac conditions in which MRI is especially useful

1. Myocarditis and cardiomyopathies: CMR can identify inflammatory changes or structural changes in the heart muscle.

2. Myocardial infarction: MRI can detect, at an early stage, the heart muscle damage that results in the death of heart muscle cells.

3. Heart valve disorders: CMR can precisely detect valve dysfunction or structural abnormalities of the valves.

4. Congenital heart disease: congenital heart defects, such as ventricular or atrial septal defects, can be detected and characterized.

## Future outlook

As MRI technology keeps developing and sequence techniques expand, CMR is expected to provide even more accurate and detailed images of cardiovascular structures and function. With artificial intelligence and image processing technologies, the diagnostic process may become even faster and more precise.

Cardiovascular MRI has radically transformed cardiac diagnostics, allowing a deeper and more detailed examination of the heart and blood vessels. As the technology continues to develop, CMR is expected to remain an essential tool in diagnosing and treating cardiovascular disease.
