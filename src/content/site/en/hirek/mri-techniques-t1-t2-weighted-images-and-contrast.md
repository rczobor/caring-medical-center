MRI is an innovative technology that uses a combination of magnetic fields and radio waves to visualize the structures inside the body. There are many variants and modes of MRI imaging, each useful from a different clinical point of view. In this chapter, we look at the basic techniques related to T1- and T2-weighted images and the use of contrast agents, and at their clinical applications.

## T1- and T2-weighted images: what is relaxation time?

Understanding relaxation time is key to understanding MRI techniques. In short, relaxation time is the time it takes for atomic nuclei to return from the excited state created by the magnetic field to their normal state. In this context, we distinguish two main relaxation times: T1 (longitudinal relaxation time) and T2 (transverse relaxation time).

1. T1-weighted images: These images highlight areas where the T1 relaxation time is short. On these images, fatty tissue usually appears bright, while water-rich tissue appears dark. They are often used for anatomical information, for example to tell apart the white and gray matter of the brain.

2. T2-weighted images: These images highlight areas where the T2 relaxation time is long. On T2-weighted images, water-rich areas (such as edema) appear bright, while fatty tissue appears dark. They are often used to identify abnormal conditions such as brain lesions, joint inflammation or tumors.

## Contrast agents in MRI

Contrast agents used in MRI are compounds given to increase the contrast of the images and so make abnormalities easier to identify. The most commonly used MRI contrast agents are gadolinium-based.

1. Why is a contrast agent used? In some areas, natural contrast is not enough to identify abnormalities accurately. A contrast agent can help make tumors, inflammatory processes or vascular abnormalities more visible.

2. When is it used? A contrast agent is often used when T1- or T2-weighted images alone do not give enough information. Examples include brain tumors, examining the integrity of blood vessel walls, and acute inflammatory processes.

3. Safety considerations: Although gadolinium-based contrast agents are generally well tolerated, side effects or allergic reactions can rarely occur. In addition, contrast agents must be used with caution in patients with impaired kidney function.

Understanding MRI techniques helps doctors provide patients with the best possible diagnostic information. The different modes of MRI imaging and the use of contrast agents let clinicians get a detailed and accurate picture of abnormalities, so they can recommend the most suitable treatment. As MRI technology develops, these imaging techniques will be refined even further, giving doctors an even more accurate picture of diseases and disorders.
