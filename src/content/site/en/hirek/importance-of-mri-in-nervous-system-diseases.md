The nervous system, which includes the brain and the spinal cord, is one of the most complex and sensitive systems of the human body. Functional and structural disorders of this system are often complicated and complex. The role of MRI in recognizing diseases of the nervous system has changed radically over the past decades, and today it is one of the most important tools in neurological diagnostics.

## The advantages of MRI in nervous system exams

MRI can make high-resolution images of the tissues of the brain and spinal cord. Because these tissues are largely made of water, MRI is especially good at telling the different structures of the nervous system apart.

1. Anatomical detail: MRI makes the fine anatomical structures of the brain visible, such as the cerebral cortex, the ventricles of the brain and the white matter.

2. No ionizing radiation: Because MRI does not use X-rays, there is no risk of radiation exposure.

3. Contrast imaging: T1- and T2-weighted images, together with contrast agents, allow accurate imaging of various neurological disorders, such as tumors, inflammation or atherosclerosis.

## Neurological disorders and MRI

1. Multiple sclerosis (MS): MS is a chronic autoimmune disease that damages the myelin sheath around nerve fibers. With MRI, doctors can identify and track areas of myelin damage in the brain and spinal cord.

2. Brain tumors: MRI lets us determine whether a brain tumor is present and where it is. A contrast agent is often used to define the exact borders of the tumor and how it relates to the surrounding tissue.

3. Stroke: A stroke happens when a blood vessel in the brain is blocked or bleeds. MRI can quickly identify the damaged areas, allowing early intervention and better-tailored treatment.

4. Traumatic brain injuries: MRI can show traumatic brain injuries, such as bleeding (hematomas) in the brain or damage to nerve fibers.

MRI has brought a revolutionary change in diagnosing diseases of the nervous system. With sharp, detailed images and contrast imaging, doctors can identify neurological disorders more accurately and faster, and so give patients more effective treatment. As MRI technology keeps developing, it will offer even more accurate images and better diagnostic options across a wide range of nervous system diseases.
