## Stress fracture (fatigue fracture)

- It often shows up as **subacute, dull pain** that eases with rest and gets worse with load.
- It mainly affects **young adult athletes** (track and field, running, basketball), but it also occurs **in older people**, under load or alongside osteoporosis.
- The early stage rarely shows on an X-ray, so **MRI** is recommended: it is highly sensitive and shows **edema along the bone trabeculae and tiny cracks**.

## What happens in the body with a stress fracture

- Long-term microtrauma → overuse inflammation, disrupted bone remodeling.
- Periosteal irritation, edema → the start of bone injury.
- Overload **does not let the bone recover**, so the stress keeps increasing the number of microfractures.

## Effective treatment: support from PEMF and laser

1. **PEMF (pulsed magnetic field)**

- In randomized studies, it has been shown to improve the chance of fracture healing 1.2-fold and to reduce pain.
- PEMF stimulates osteoblasts, improves angiogenesis (carboxyl factors) and speeds up consolidation.
- An FDA-approved treatment for non-union cases

## 2. Laser therapy

- Increases fibroblast and bone cell activity, and eases inflammation and pain.
- Can be combined with PEMF so that the two support each other's effect.

## 3. Additional options — based on international recommendations

- **LIPUS** (low-intensity pulsed ultrasound): a proven effect in speeding up fracture healing [Tesla SPMF](https://www.teslaspemf.com/en/about-5-1).
- **Extracorporeal shockwave therapy (ESWT)**: used mainly to treat sacral stress fractures; it promotes angiogenesis and osteogenesis [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/articles/PMC3279068).

## Complex service package: recommended by Caring Medical's engineers

## 1. Diagnostic block

- Medical examination + medical history
- MRI stress protocol (T2-STIR) — early diagnosis
- X-ray + ultrasound (if relevant)

## 2. Therapy block

- **PEMF**: 2–4 hours a day, in a 6–12 week cycle (depending on the case)
- **Laser therapy**: 2–3 sessions a week, 10–12 treatments
- **Optional: LIPUS or ESWT** (in more severe cases, depending on available equipment)
- **Physiotherapy / exercise therapy**: progressive loading, biomechanical correction

## 3. Follow-up

- MRI: after 6–8 weeks, to monitor healing
- Functional status assessment (pain, load tolerance, return plan)

## Benefits for patients

**Early diagnosis** – a complete fracture and complications can be avoided. **Faster healing**, pain relief. Less need for surgery. Supported, documented care with an institutional background.
