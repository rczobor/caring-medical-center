The musculoskeletal system – including the muscles, bones, joints and surrounding soft tissues – plays a key role in moving the body and maintaining its shape. When these structures stop working properly, it can cause serious pain and limited mobility. MRI is one of the leading tools for diagnosing and treating such disorders.

## Benefits of MRI diagnostics in examining the musculoskeletal system

MRI is a non-invasive method that produces detailed, three-dimensional images of the body's internal structures. It can detect damage and changes in soft tissues, joints, ligaments, cartilage and other structures.

1. No radiation exposure: Because MRI does not use ionizing radiation, unlike CT or X-ray, it can be repeated without exposing the patient to radiation.

2. Contrast and resolution: MRI produces exceptionally detailed images of soft tissues, which makes it possible to detect smaller changes, such as microscopic tears or inflammation.

## Examining joints and ligaments

Athletes and people with an active lifestyle often suffer joint or ligament injuries. MRI can quickly and accurately identify the extent and location of an injury, for example:

1. Cartilage injuries: MRI can detect damage to or wear of cartilage, which is usually a sign of osteoarthritis or other degenerative joint diseases.

2. Ligament injuries: Ligament tears and sprains are common, especially in the knee and ankle. MRI helps identify the extent of the injury and choose the most suitable treatment options.

## Muscles and soft tissues

Muscle injuries – such as strains, bruises or tears – often happen during physical activity. MRI helps with:

1. Muscle damage: MRI can detect small muscle tears or bruises, even before symptoms appear.

2. Inflammation and infection: MRI can detect inflammation or infection in soft tissues and muscles, allowing fast diagnosis and treatment.

MRI plays an essential role in diagnosing diseases and injuries of the musculoskeletal system. It gives a detailed and accurate picture of the condition of joints, muscles, ligaments and other soft tissues, allowing doctors to choose the best possible treatment strategy for their patients. Continuous advances in the technology keep improving the effectiveness and accuracy of MRI diagnostics in treating diseases and injuries of the musculoskeletal system.
