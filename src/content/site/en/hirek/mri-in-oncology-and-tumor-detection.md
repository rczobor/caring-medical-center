Oncology is the study and treatment of cancer. In cancer, the body's cells divide and grow uncontrollably. MRI is one of the key tools in oncology, because it makes it possible to detect tumors, determine their size and location, and monitor how well treatment is working.

## Detecting and characterizing tumors

MRI lets doctors see tumors and their surroundings. High-resolution images and different weightings allow oncologists to diagnose tumors accurately and to draw up a treatment plan.

1. Contrast agents: MRI contrast agents make the outlines of tumors, and the relationship between tumors and the surrounding tissue, more visible. They also help examine a tumor's blood supply, which is important information for understanding the activity of the cancer cells and how aggressive the tumor is.

2. Differential diagnosis: MRI can help tell benign and malignant tumors apart, and identify the type and histological nature of a tumor.

## Planning and assessing treatment

MRI is not only important for diagnosis. Knowing the exact location and size of a tumor is essential for planning surgery and radiotherapy.

1. Surgical planning: MRI gives a detailed picture of the tumor and its position relative to the surrounding organs and structures. Based on this, surgeons can plan the procedure in advance and minimize the risks.

2. Radiotherapy: MRI allows radiation to be targeted precisely, minimizing damage to healthy tissue.

3. Assessing treatment: with MRI scans after treatment, doctors can assess how effective the treatment was, and see whether the tumor has shrunk or the activity of the tumor cells has decreased.

Thanks to MRI, patients in oncology get a faster and more accurate diagnosis, which greatly improves treatment success and survival rates. As MRI technology continues to develop and new imaging techniques become available, oncologists will get even more precise information about tumors and be able to offer patients even more effective treatments.
