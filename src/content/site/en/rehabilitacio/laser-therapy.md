Laser therapy is one of the most advanced and most effective methods in modern physical therapy for relieving pain, reducing inflammation and regenerating tissue. During the treatment, your cells absorb light energy, which triggers biochemical processes. This speeds up healing and reduces pain, whether the condition is acute or chronic.

## What happens during laser therapy?

The focused, coherent light of the laser beam penetrates deep into the tissues and has a biostimulating effect. This means that the cells' energy production (ATP synthesis) increases, which stimulates regeneration and circulation and reduces inflammatory mediators. The result: faster tissue healing, less pain and swelling, muscle relaxation and faster wound healing.

## Therapeutic effects of laser therapy:

- Pain relief: the laser light blocks the transmission of pain signals along the nerve pathways.
- Anti-inflammatory effect: it reduces the activity of inflammatory cells and increases local microcirculation.
- Regeneration: it stimulates cell division and collagen production, speeding up tissue regeneration
- Muscle relaxation: it reduces tension and releases cramped muscles.
- Wound healing: it boosts cell metabolism and improves blood and lymph circulation, which helps new skin form.

## Areas of use

- Sports injuries: muscle strains, tendon injuries, bruises
- Joint and soft tissue pain: tennis elbow, shoulder pain, knee pain
- Inflammatory conditions: tendinitis, bursitis, plantar fasciitis
- Wound healing: surgical scars, ulcers, burns
- Chronic spine problems, lower back pain, neck pain

## Contraindications – when can it not be used?

- Directly over a malignant tumor
- During pregnancy, on the abdomen and pelvis
- Active infections, fever
- If you take a medication that causes light sensitivity
- Thyroid and the area around the eyes – direct exposure is prohibited
