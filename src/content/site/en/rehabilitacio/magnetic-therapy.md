Magnetic therapy can be used for pain at any stage. The treatment is based on three different pain control theories, each with a different frequency spectrum. This is why the treatment brings immediate relief, whether the condition is chronic or acute. Besides pain relief, its therapeutic effects include fracture healing, muscle relaxation and joint mobilization.

## What happens during a muscle injury?

An injury causes not only pain but also a biological crisis. Our blog article “What happens during a muscle injury – and how can magnetic therapy help?” covers this.

## Areas of use for magnetic therapy:

- Sports injuries – fractures, ligament injuries, muscle injuries, tendon injuries
- Overuse injuries – muscle inflammation, tendinitis
- Lower back pain
- Tunnel syndromes
- Degenerative joint changes

## Why is it cutting-edge technology?

Magnetic therapy uses a unique, high-intensity electromagnetic field with a strength of 3 or 7 tesla. The electromagnetic field, induced by a coil inside the applicator, interacts with the human body and depolarizes neuromuscular tissue. Depending on the stimulation frequency and field intensity selected, it can relieve pain, free up a stiff joint, support fracture healing, or relax or strengthen muscles.

- Because magnetic therapy targets neuromuscular tissue, it can be used to treat conditions of the musculoskeletal and nervous systems that cause chronic or acute pain.
- At certain settings, the interaction between the magnetic field and the muscle leads to beneficial muscle contractions. Repeated contractions of the muscles around a joint can free up a stiff joint.
- Because it improves local blood circulation and tissue metabolism, magnetic therapy is recommended in fracture care from the start of treatment.
- To strengthen weakened muscles, magnetic therapy triggers action potentials in neuromuscular tissue, which causes muscle contractions.
- With high-frequency stimulation, magnetic therapy effectively reduces muscle tension, so it can also be used to treat spastic disorders.

## Magnetic therapy (PEMF) in treatment

## 1. What is PEMF and how does it work?

PEMF (pulsed electromagnetic field therapy) uses short, alternating magnetic pulses that stimulate regeneration at the cellular level.

## Clinical effects:

- [Reduces pain](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/magnets-for-pain-what-you-need-to-know) and inflammation in **wear-and-tear and degenerative joint changes**, such as knee and hand osteoarthritis.
- Improves healing of **soft tissue injuries** (sprains, strains).
- Helps **bone regeneration**, for example by supporting fracture healing.
- Increases **microcirculation and angiogenesis**, which improves oxygen supply.

## Physiological mechanisms:

- Increases cell membrane permeability → more efficient exchange of nutrients and waste products.
- Increases NO production → wider blood vessels and better circulation.
- Stimulates MSC differentiation (stem cells → chondrocytes) → cartilage surface regeneration [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10971695/).

## 3. When is it not recommended? – [contraindications](https://itechmedicaldivision.com/en/contraindications-pemf-therapy/)

- **Pregnancy**, because there are no studies supporting its safety.
- **Implanted electronic devices** (e.g. pacemaker, insulin pump): it may interfere with how they work
- **Children who are still growing**, because its effect on bone development is unknown.
- Active **tumor**, **severe heart rhythm disorders**, **acute infections**: best avoided.
- Based on partial evidence: not recommended if you have a stable **metal implant**.

## 4. How does the treatment work?

- **Frequency**: 5–50 Hz; **flux density**: 0.1–30 mT (for deep-penetration devices)
- **Duration**: 30–60 minutes a day, 3–7 times a week, for 6–12 weeks.
- In simple local or whole-body treatment units (mat, coil).

## 5. Safety and side effects

- PEMF is **well tolerated**, with [**no significant side effects**](https://itechmedicaldivision.com/en/contraindications-pemf-therapy/).
- In rare cases of sensitivity, a local burning sensation may occur.
- Safe combination: a non-ionizing, non-infrared procedure that does not enter body cavities.
